Motivation

Electricity prices
are volatile.
Most factories ignore it.

  • German EPEX SPOT prices range from −€500 to +€800/MWh within a single day
  • Industrial consumers with flexible load can shift demand to low-price windows — but need forecasts to act
  • Without probabilistic planning, a single wrong DR decision costs more than doing nothing

Step 1

Feature Engineering

Time Encoding
  • hour_sin / hour_cos — cyclic encoding, avoids 23→0 discontinuity
  • day_of_week, month, is_weekend
  • 24h & 168h lag prices (price memory)
  • Ablated: raw integer hour degrades MAE
Calendar & Regime
  • is_public_holiday + lead-24h flag
  • is_christmas_week — Dec 24–Jan 2 low-demand period
  • negative_price_dummy_lag_24 — yesterday had negative price
  • holiday_load_window (interaction term)
Market Elasticity
  • rolling_elasticity — 168h rolling OLS slope of price on load
  • Captures local supply-demand sensitivity
  • Renewable lag features: wind_lag_24, solar_lag_24
  • Load lags: load_lag_24, load_lag_168

Step 3 — Uncertainty

Block Bootstrap
Scenario Fan

  • 48 scenarios per day — each a complete 24-hour price/load/renewable path
  • Block length = 24h — residuals sampled as full-day blocks, preserving intra-day autocorrelation
  • Residual history from calibrated forecast errors on the training set
  • Why not IID? Ablation shows block bootstrap gives 72.7% vs IID 69.4% P10–P90 coverage

Step 4 — Decision

DR Optimization
Algorithm

  • Objective: minimize average cost across all 48 scenarios weighted by scenario probability
  • Counterfactual price model: Ridge + RF structural model predicts post-DR price from demand change
  • Flexibility constraint: 10% of baseline demand, min_load_ratio = 70%
  • Negative-price guardrail: suppresses DR when P(price < threshold) ≥ 30% — prevents reducing load into price spikes
① Scenario Grid
48 price scenarios × 24 hours × flexibility steps
↓
② Guardrail Check
If P(price < low_threshold) ≥ 0.30 → block DR for that hour
↓
③ Counterfactual Price
Ridge+RF model: price_cf = f(demand_base − q_DR, renewables, time)
↓
④ Optimal Schedule
q_DA_optimal per hour → day_ahead_optimal_dr_schedule.csv

Level 4 Evaluation

Mechanism Validity — 8/8 Passed

Price–Demand Elasticity: Demand reduction leads to counterfactual price decrease — structural sign correct
DR Targeting: Optimizer selects high-price hours with statistically higher prices than non-DR hours
Guardrail Activation: Negative-price guard correctly suppresses DR for hours with P(neg) ≥ 0.30
Flexibility Constraint: DR quantity never exceeds flexibility_ratio × demand_base in any hour
Min-Load Constraint: Residual demand always ≥ 70% of baseline — load floor enforced
Scenario Uncertainty: Wider P10–P90 intervals correlate with lower DR utilization — model is risk-aware
Counterfactual Direction: Post-DR counterfactual price always ≤ observed price when DR applied
No Negative-Price DR: Zero hours where DR was applied at negative prices (guardrail effective)

Summary

Four Key Findings

1 Scenarios are the most critical module. Removing them drops cost reduction by 27.5% — probabilistic planning is not optional.
2 XGBoost outperforms all baselines. 43% lower MAE than naïve, 15% lower than RF — justified by benchmark across 4 models.
3 All 4 evaluation levels pass. L1 accuracy → L2 scenario quality → L3 decision performance → L4 mechanism validity: 8/8 checks, 0% violations.
4 The pipeline is production-ready. FastAPI layer + Claude preprocessing agent + config-driven model selection = deployable B2B SaaS.
